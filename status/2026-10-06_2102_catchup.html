The machinery every stage uses

Paid versus organic Fenix clients

A Fenix (Firefox for Android) client is paid if the client-level table mozdata.fenix.new_profile_clients records it as acquired through a paid Google click, on the release channel, installed from the Play Store. Everything else is organic by residual. Only Fenix has paid traffic; Firefox iOS and the two Focus apps are treated as 100% organic. Iran is excluded from the paid treatment (98.8% organic, and marketing's curve is ex-Iran).

The measured split and the two-piece add-back

Once per cycle a producer script builds fenix_paid_organic.<seam>.parquet: the organic share of Fenix DAU per day and country, measured from 2024-06-01 (the retention limit of the client table) to the day before the seam. Mobile DAU trains from 2020-12-31, so for the roughly 3.5 years before 2024-06-01 each country's earliest measured share is held flat backwards (the held-flat backfill; bounded at about 1.1 percentage points because paid was only 1.1% of Fenix DAU at the oldest measured month). Before the model runs, that share multiplies the Fenix training rows so mozaic sees organic DAU only. After the model runs, paid comes back in two pieces. Training rows get the measured paid, so they return to the raw actuals exactly. Forecast rows get marketing's paid level, allocated across countries by each country's share of measured paid over the trailing 28 days. The design exists so that paid contributes exactly its own value to the forecast, with no interaction with Prophet's trend, as the display-layer headwinds do.

Why there is a mirror snapshot and a tail

The share comes from a scratch BigQuery table, the mirror (moz-fx-data-bq-data-science.brwells.fenix_dau_growth_source_v1), built on 2026-07-01 from the client-level tables and expiring 2027-04-01. The client-level source keeps only about 25 months, so the mirror is what preserves history back to 2024-06-01. Each cycle the producer appends a tail: the same query run live from 2026-06-25 to the day before the seam, so snapshot and tail overlap for seven days. The tail-overlap check compares total DAU on those seven days and refuses to concatenate if they disagree by more than a tolerance, on the theory that disagreement means the tail SQL has drifted from the mirror build.

The paid seam step

Measured paid on the last training day and marketing's level on the first forecast day are two different measurements of the same quantity and never agree exactly. The difference is the paid seam step. It is reported after every build and never smoothed. It is quoted two ways: daily (last measured day versus first marketing day) and on 28-day means (measured trailing mean versus marketing's first day). It depends on the seam and on which paid curve is wired, so it must be re-measured after every refresh.

28-day trailing moving average

Every published mobile curve, including the paid-only plot, is a trailing 28-day mean of a daily series. A trailing mean reacts to a level change over 28 days: on the day after the change the window holds 27 old days and one new, and it takes four weeks for the window to hold only new days. Keep this in mind when reading any curve near the seam.

Stages

1. Replace the marketing-lift overlay with the measured split

August 2026 cycle · seam 2026-07-28, refreshed to 2026-08-02

Assumption under test

That paid mobile DAU can be measured from the client-level paid/organic flag and stacked on an organic-only forecast, instead of being modelled as an increment since an arbitrary anchor date (the retired code m).

What we built

The producer scripts/build_fenix_organic_split.py (mirror snapshot plus tail, four checks: tail overlap, partition identity, split coverage, shredder drift), the consumer module src/mozaic_daily/organic.py, the spec data-official/2026-08/organic/organic.json, and the research record in research/mobile-organic/. Marketing's paid input was the two single-channel feeds, stored as a lift relative to 2026-03-30 with the anchor value (922,250) carried in the spec and added back, a framing inherited from m.

What we tested against

An external prototype in ~/work/product-data-science-core/scratch/brwells/mobile_organic_aug/ (a different repository, outside mozaic-daily; reproduced, not ported), the raw actuals (training rows must return to them byte for byte), and the production level source (the shredder-drift check, named for the shredder, the data-platform job that deletes a client's rows from client-level tables when the user asks for deletion: the mirror loses about 2.9% to such deletions over 26 months relative to the production aggregate, so the share comes from the mirror but the level always comes from production).

What we found

The old m overlay absorbed 58% of any change to its curve (a −141,653 Dec-15 lift change moved the KPI only −59,875), so a lift-curve comparison had never been a KPI estimate. Under p paid contributes exactly its own value. The swap cost 322,714 at mobile Dec-15, and a 33-probe parameter search across three seasonality regimes could not recover it. The paid seam step at the 2026-08-02 seam was +1,903 per day (+0.01%).

What we decided

Adopt p for mobile from August on and retire m (it stays registered so July's artifacts load). Add the mobile tailwind t (+299,000 at Dec-15) as its own display-layer code to carry the remainder of the swap cost; about 47% of it was a measured excess from an independent implementation, about 45% a planning decision, about 8% calibration to July's published number. Backfilled into the decisions log today as D-015 and D-016 from data-official/2026-08/organic/_index.md and data-official/2026-08/tailwind/_index.md; the dates are the cycle's, not the day the choice was made.

3. October rebuild: the overlap check fails, and the paid curve falls behind measured paid

October 2026 cycle · seam 2026-10-05 · 6 October (today)

Assumption under test

That the September machinery carries forward unchanged to a new seam: rebuild the split, keep the Med paid curve, rerun the model. Two parts of that assumption broke.

What we built

The October split data-official/2026-10/organic/fenix_paid_organic.2026-10-05.parquet (856 days × 16 countries, the production mobile country set of 15 named markets plus ROW, with Iran excluded later by the applier; 392 GB scanned), a new --overlap-tolerance flag on the producer (default still 0.5%) with a unit test, the October mobile build data-official/2026-10/mobile_cpr0725_paid0915med_2026-10-05/ (.adj-p.; the directory name encodes the model's changepoint range 0.725, the paid curve pulled on 09-15 (Med), and the seam; the full parameter lock is in its parameters.json), and the paid/organic plots in data-official/2026-10/plots/.

What we tested against

The producer's four checks; the training-rows-equal-actuals verifier (exact on all 61 probed days); the app breakdown (ALL MOBILE equals the sum of the four apps); and, for the failed check, a re-run of both sides of the overlap for just the seven overlap days, broken down by paid bucket.

What went wrong

The tail-overlap check failed at 0.5153% on 2026-06-25 against a 0.50% tolerance. September had passed the same check at 0.347% with a median of −0.33%, so the gap had grown, not appeared. Re-running the overlap by bucket showed where it comes from: paid clients acquired more than a year earlier have lost a quarter of their DAU in the live tables since July, while organic is unchanged. This is client deletion in the live tables, the same mechanism the shredder-drift check already tolerates, and the check's error text ("the tail SQL has drifted") names the wrong cause.

On 2026-10-05 the 28-day window still holds 27 measured days (around 1.80M to 1.99M each) and one marketing day (1.68M), so the mean barely moves: 1,800,407. Each day after that, one measured day leaves the window and one marketing day enters. Every marketing day is about 100,000 to 200,000 lower than the measured day it replaces, so the mean falls: 1,790,751 on 10-12, 1,772,673 on 10-19, 1,744,379 on 10-26, and 1,709,948 on 11-01, when the window holds only marketing days. From 11-01 the green line is the 28-day mean of the Med file. So is the blue line, because September and October wired the identical file, and that is why they coincide from 1 November to the end of the year (both 1,804,995 at Dec-15). The blue line went the other way from its own seam because in September the Med curve (1,644,163 on 09-09) sat above the trailing measured mean (1,611,344), so swapping measured days for marketing days pushed its mean up.

The fall, then, is not a forecast of paid declining. It is the trailing mean draining out four weeks of measured paid that run higher than the curve marketing published in early September. Measured paid has outgrown the Med scenario by roughly 12% in four weeks (the 1,890,626 seven-day mean against the curve's 1,684,949 at the seam), and the curve needs re-pulling. The dashed lines add the t ramp (0 at each seam, +299,000 at Dec-15) to the solid lines; they are drawn because about half of t was sized from a paid excess, so paid-plus-t is the closest thing on the chart to a "paid including the tailwind" view.
