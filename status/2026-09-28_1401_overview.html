What this is and why

Mozilla publishes an official forecast of Firefox daily active users (DAU) for desktop and mobile every month. This repository produces it. The forecast runs daily from the present to the end of 2027, but leadership plans against one number from it: the 28-day average of world DAU on 15 December 2026, per platform, judged against three 2026 planning targets called Low, Baseline and Stretch. Each monthly run, called a cycle, refits a statistical model on the latest telemetry, layers on a small set of named corrections (adjustments) for things the model cannot see, and publishes the curves to a KPI spreadsheet and dashboard. The September 2026 cycle is the current one. Its job was to refit on September data, carry August's adjustments forward, add three new ones, and land a December figure leadership would sign off on.

Where it stands

The September numbers are final in the sense that matters here: leadership signed them off on 15 September and the repository holds them as the cycle's published curves. The all-platform total clears all three planning targets, but only because mobile runs well above its own: desktop lands below its Baseline and Stretch targets, and the total's margin over Stretch is thin. Two steps after that are unconfirmed: whether the numbers were pasted into the KPI spreadsheet that stakeholders read, and whether the cycle was formally closed out; neither is recorded. The last change to the repository was on 17 September. Since then, a separate prototype of the 2027 forecast (same author, different repository, exploring next year's method) has produced a claim, not yet verified in this repository, that paid mobile users are being under-identified. It comes from another agent's work and has not been checked here. It does not change September, which was built before it; confirming or refuting it is a question for the October cycle.

How it works
  • A raw model first. An in-house package called Mozaic fits a Prophet time-series model for each country, then reconciles those country forecasts so they sum to the world total, separately for desktop and mobile.
  • Then named adjustments. Each corrects for something the model cannot see and is known by a one-letter code. A headwind lowers the forecast, a tailwind raises it. Eight are active this cycle and one is built but switched off. Three are new in September: j, i and u; the rest carry over from August.
CodeWhat it corrects for
hDesktop users lost as Windows 10 leaves support. Headwind, the largest adjustment.
pPaid mobile acquisition: the model forecasts organic users only and marketing's paid forecast is added on top (see the next bullet).
l, e"Launch at Login", a feature that starts Firefox when the computer logs in, lifting desktop use. One curve for new users (l), one for existing users (e, built but switched off).
oUsers of a Chinese distribution partner migrating onto mainline Firefox. Tailwind.
jAutomated (bot) desktop traffic in Japan, kept out of the trend.
iIndia desktop use running above a typical year.
t, uTwo mobile corrections: a positive calibration (t, carried from August, adopted to keep the mobile forecast in line with the previous cycle's published level after a method change) and a small headwind for users lost to the 2025 terms-of-use requirement (u, new).
  • Two kinds of adjustment. Some are built into the model fit, so changing them means a rerun. Others are applied to the final curve and can be changed instantly.
  • Paid mobile is measured, not modelled. Users of Firefox for Android are classed as paid or organic by an ad-click identifier in their attribution data. The model forecasts organic users only, and the marketing team's own paid forecast is stacked on top.
  • The seam moves each cycle. The seam is the forecast start date, the day after the last day of real data; here 9 September. Every adjustment is re-aligned to it.
  • Calibration is explicit. Where leadership or the team chose a number rather than measuring one, the choice is recorded as its own adjustment or anchor, so judgement never hides inside a measurement.
  • Every build is recorded. Every published forecast carries a record of its settings and adjustments, and older builds are kept so changes can be undone.

Record of work

Chronological, one short paragraph per stage. Decisions cite the log in status/decisions.md.

Cycle opened and inputs ingested

4 September

August was closed out and the September branch opened. Three new desktop adjustments were brought in from delivered files (Japan bots, India excess, a refreshed China migration curve), the mobile terms-of-use headwind became its own code, and draft forecasts ran at a 2 September seam (D-001, D-002).

Headwind calibrated, seam refreshed

8 to 10 September

The Windows 10 headwind was set so desktop landed a chosen distance above August. The seam moved to 9 September with fresh data and the headwind was re-set to hold that distance (D-003 to D-006).

Paid-curve variants and prediction intervals

10 to 11 September

The marketing team delivered a workbook of paid-DAU scenarios; the Low scenario was adopted first. Prediction intervals were computed for the raw models. A further desktop tailwind was built and withheld (D-007).

Leadership review

15 September

Leadership asked for a smaller Windows 10 headwind and a switch from the Low to the Med paid scenario. Mobile was rerun; the published numbers became the ones in the table above (D-008).

Records, exports, archive

17 September

The per-adjustment effects record was built, the KPI spreadsheet replacement file was generated, a daily unsmoothed desktop export was written (D-009), and the whole cycle was archived to cloud storage without deletions.
